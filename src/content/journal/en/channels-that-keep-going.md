I choose the videos that make up my channels, but I want to be able to forget that selection once I am in front of the television. Turn the knob, arrive halfway through a concert, stay for a while or move on to something else: that is the way of watching I am interested in. I want a stream that does not wait for me to be there before it starts.

Each channel on the deck has a playlist of videos chosen in advance. Time moves forward virtually on every channel, including the ones I am not watching. If I leave one and return a few minutes later, its programme has continued. That brings back some of the surprise of broadcast television, with content I want to watch.

## Make the channels, then let them run

I created the Deck Manager to configure the system and put playlists together. It lets me prepare the programming in advance; when it is time to watch, the tuning knob gives me access to whatever is playing on each channel. Frequency selection, channel simulation and the effects already work in the application.

The band can change with my interests. I can add a playlist to create a new channel, expand the programming and then return to that television experience. I am not trying to settle on a permanent schedule: I choose the content, then let time determine where I find it.

## Music comes first

At home, the deck will mostly play music. It is my passion, and the pictures should help set the mood in the living room. Sade was the starting point; around that first playlist, I have several other ways of spending time with music in mind.

One channel will play complete televised concerts from the 1970s, roughly an hour each, one after another. Stevie Wonder, Curtis Mayfield, Gil Scott-Heron: each in a separate performance from the period. I want to come across a live show partway through and stay with it, rather than keep only a few songs.

I also want programmes such as _Soul Train_ or Cher’s prime-time shows, and a channel of 1980s pop videos. I am drawn to the graphic style of those videos and the way their pictures work on a small 4:3 screen. Their relationship with television is part of what I look for when choosing them for this equipment.

## A few other places to land

The other channels are there for the occasional pleasure of finding something different, and to make the band feel like a fuller television landscape. I plan to have one channel of American TV series opening titles and another with anime in French, including _City Hunter_ and _Cat’s Eye_.

I grew up in France in the late 1980s and early 1990s. Those series are part of the pictures I remember from childhood and, for me, carry the atmosphere I want to bring back. They will have a place among the music programmes, even though music will remain the main reason I use the deck.

## Looking for a channel

Moving between channels matters as much as what is on them. Static and jumping-channel effects accompany the search with the tuning knob. I want to keep that moment of travelling across the band before settling on a picture.

The pointer moves above UHF markings, frequencies in MHz and channel numbers. Short, four-letter channel names sit alongside them, like AM station names on old radio receivers. I describe that idea in the note on [the UHF scale](/journal/uhf-tuning-scale/).

At the end of the band, the final channel is reserved for the Philips PM5544 test card and its tone. It, too, belongs to the television experience I want to recreate.

## One small exception

At startup, I plan to align the programming with the device’s clock, so I can join a programme already in progress when I switch it on.

I also plan to allow a jump to the next video in a playlist. It is a deliberate exception to the simulation: a way to change videos without leaving the channel. But I do not want controls for seeking forwards or backwards within a video. I keep the choice of changing the programme while letting the one I watch run its course.
